# Terrain noise primitives (P4.3a)

`src/argusnet/world/procedural.py` → `rust/argusnet-world/src/noise.rs`.

The first slice of P4.3. These seven functions — `_stable_seed`,
`_grid_values`, `_smoothstep`, `_hash_noise`, `_value_noise`, `_fbm` and
`_line_gaussian` — sit at the bottom of the terrain dependency graph. Height
shaping, hydrology masks, semantic masks and land cover all run through them,
so a one-ULP divergence here is a divergence in **every terrain the runtime
builds**. Everything is pinned bit-for-bit; nothing is compared inside a
tolerance.

`tests/fixtures/noise.json`, regenerated by
`scripts/capture_noise_fixture.py`: 13 seeds, 10 smoothsteps, 10 hashes, 12
noise samples, 9 fBm evaluations, 6 line envelopes and 7 grid axis pairs.

## Three traps, each of which produces plausible, deterministic, wrong terrain

### 1. The seed function is not the one already ported

`procedural.py::_stable_seed` is **blake2b-64, little-endian, masked to 31
bits**. `sim.py::_stable_seed` — which `argusnet_sim::deterministic::stable_seed`
already ports — is an **FNV-1 walk over characters**. They share a name and
nothing else.

Reaching for the existing function is the obvious move and it is wrong. The
parity suite has a dedicated test, `the_terrain_seed_is_not_the_fnv_walk_of_the_same_name`,
that fails if the two are ever "unified", so the mistake surfaces before any
terrain silently changes. This is why the port adds the `blake2` crate rather
than reusing what is there.

### 2. numpy wraps on `int64` overflow where Rust panics

`_hash_noise` multiplies `int64` values by `0x45D9F3B` twice. The products
exceed `i64::MAX` for ordinary grid coordinates — four of the ten fixture
cases overflow, including `(1000, 2000)`. numpy wraps silently; Rust's `*`
**panics in a debug build**. Every multiply in the port is `wrapping_mul`, and
`the_hash_fixture_still_overflows_i64` fails if the fixture ever stops
exercising it.

**A correction worth recording.** An earlier version of these notes said the
*arithmetic* right shifts were load-bearing, because half the inputs reach them
with a negative `h`. That is wrong: swapping either shift for a logical one
**cannot change the result**. Only bits 51 and above differ between the two,
multiplication propagates carries upward only, and the final `& 0xFFFF` keeps
bits that depend on operand bits 0..28. Checked over 300,000 random inputs,
149,893 of them with a negative `h`: zero disagreements. The `hash/logical-shift`
mutant is therefore the one survivor of the mutation pass, and it is a proven
equivalent rather than a gap.

### 3. `np.arange` is not `start + i * step`

numpy's `DOUBLE_fill` writes the first two elements directly, **recomputes** the
delta from them, and generates the rest from that:

```text
buf[0] = start
buf[1] = start + step
delta  = buf[1] - buf[0]        // generally != step
buf[i] = start + i * delta      // i >= 2
```

The recomputed delta is the whole point, and it is not a small effect:
`start + i * step` disagrees with numpy on about **78%** of random inputs, and
plain cumulative addition on about **80%**. The model above matched numpy 2.4.6
on **198,991 of 198,991** random cases.

Two things were checked before settling on this rather than declaring a
re-baseline. The output is **stable** with AVX2 and AVX-512 dispatch disabled,
so it is not a SIMD artifact. And numpy's value is *further* from the
correctly-rounded exact result than the naive multiply is — which is what
initially suggested, wrongly, that no formula existed. There is a formula; the
guard now requires both wrong spellings to disagree with the fixture on at
least one axis.

## Mutation: 59 of 60 caught, 1 proven equivalent

The first pass caught **57**. Two survivors needed real work:

- **`hash/logical-shift`** turned out to be a **proven equivalent**, not a gap —
  see the correction above. Left in the mutation set as documentation.
- **`noise/lerp-alternate-form`** (`a + t*(b - a)` vs `(1 - t)*a + t*b`) needed
  cases found by search, and the *first* search looked in the wrong place.
  Searching on the inner `nx0` found candidates easily — and every one of them
  was **absorbed by the outer lerp**, so the fixture still pinned nothing and
  the mutant survived a second time. Searching on the **final** `value_noise`
  output has a hit rate of about **2 × 10⁻⁶** per random point: the four cases
  in the fixture came out of two million tries.

That second miss is the same failure this migration has now hit three times: a
discriminating case chosen by measuring an *intermediate* rather than the thing
the test actually asserts. The guards in both the capture script and
`noise_parity.rs` now recompute the whole function the wrong way and require a
disagreement in the final result.

One more case is pinned as a **unit test** rather than in the fixture:
`fbm`'s `amplitude_sum.max(1.0e-9)` floor. The reference cannot reach it —
`persistence` defaults to `0.52` and the sum starts at `1.0` and only grows —
but the Rust API takes it as data, and `persistence = -1` with an even octave
count makes the amplitudes cancel to exactly zero.

## Also pinned

- `_fbm`'s loop order: the amplitude sum accumulates **before** the wavelength
  and amplitude advance, and the wavelength is divided **before** the amplitude
  is scaled. Both orderings are caught.
- The per-octave seed step `+ octave * 9973`, and the `max(1.0)` wavelength and
  `max(1)` octave floors.
- `_line_gaussian`'s along-track envelope is the **fourth** power, not the
  square — a flat-topped plateau, not a bell. Both directions of that swap are
  caught.
- `_grid_values`' `+ resolution * 0.5` stop padding, and its fallback to the
  two bounds when an axis yields fewer than two samples.

## Not in this slice

Everything above the primitives: `_semantic_masks`, `procedural_terrain_grid`,
`procedural_height_grid`, `build_terrain_layer`, `build_land_cover_layer` and
`build_landscape`. They are the rest of P4.3 and they all depend on this layer
being exact first.
